# Research digest: agentic iOS apps, as of 2026-09-24

Compiled from 5 web-research passes. Confidence tags: [v]=verified, [l]=likely, [u]=uncertain. Many news sites were blocked, so some claims rest on search snippets. Treat [u] as unconfirmed.

## apple-platform

As of late September 2026, Apple has split its agent strategy into two layers. The first is a first-party orchestrator: Siri AI. It was announced at WWDC26 on June 8, 2026 and shipped September 14, 2026 as an opt-in, English-only beta with a waitlist, on iPhone 15 Pro and later, outside the EU and China. It is built on Apple Foundation Models that Google Gemini helped create (joint statement, January 12, 2026). Siri AI combines personal context, on-screen awareness and cross-app actions, but it only reaches into third-party apps through App Intents. App Schemas, entity indexing and view annotations make that possible, and press reports say SiriKit is being deprecated. The second layer is a strong toolkit for building agents inside your own app. The Foundation Models framework now has a rebuilt on-device model with a 4,096-token context and multimodal Attachment input, plus built-in OCR, barcode and Spotlight-search tools. Dynamic Profiles add agent orchestration (ToolCallingMode .required loops, baton-pass and phone-a-friend patterns, history transforms). A free PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel offers 32K context and reasoning levels, with per-user daily quotas, for apps under 2M downloads. A LanguageModel/LanguageModelExecutor protocol lets Claude, Gemini (via Firebase) or local Core AI and MLX models run behind the same session API. The platform also added LongRunningIntent, BGContinuedProcessingTask, Live Activities (push-to-start, broadcast, CarPlay/Mac/Watch surfaces), Visual Intelligence hooks, the CarPlay voice-conversation template, the Call Translation API and a WWDC26 session on securing agentic features (onToolCall confirmations, intent authenticationPolicy, spotlighting untrusted content).

The key walls for independent agent builders still stand. There is no public, shipped way for a third-party agent to call other apps' App Intents: MCP support is only groundwork seen in iOS 26.1 beta code, and it shipped in Xcode 27 as a developer bridge, not in iOS. The Apple Intelligence "Extensions" that would let Claude or Gemini power Siri are reported but not live beyond ChatGPT as of September 2026. On-screen awareness is Siri-only and depends on annotations each app adds. Background execution is still bounded: intents get 30 seconds unless they use LongRunningIntent with progress reporting, continued tasks must be user-initiated, and Foundation Models has a background rate budget. App Review blocks self-extending, code-generating apps under guideline 2.5.2 (the vibe-coding removals of March 2026) and requires explicit consent before sending personal data to third-party AI (5.1.2(i), November 2025). Apple is shipping its own vertical agents: the Passwords auto-change agent, Hold Assist and Call Screening, Shortcuts built from natural language, and Visual Intelligence in the Camera app. Meanwhile it has delayed or scaled back the Health AI coach and has shipped no agentic-payments API. That leaves clear gaps in health coaching, commerce and payments, cross-app orchestration outside Siri, and security tooling for App Intents.

### Walls
- There is no public API for one third-party app to discover or invoke another app's App Intents. Only Siri AI, or user-built Shortcuts, can orchestrate across apps. MCP for App Intents is unshipped groundwork, and Apple's MCP support is limited to Xcode 27's mcpbridge.
- Siri AI is gated: opt-in beta with a waitlist, iPhone 15 Pro or later, English only at launch, not available in the EU (DMA) or China. Most iPhone owners cannot use the system agent yet.
- Siri AI reaches third-party apps only through the new App Intents schemas, indexed entities and view annotations. SiriKit-only apps are reportedly invisible to it, and reviewers report slow Spotlight indexing and patchy third-party coverage.
- On-screen awareness is a Siri-only capability built from app-supplied annotations. Third-party apps cannot read other apps' screens or UI, and there is no computer-use or Accessibility-style automation of other apps on iOS.
- The on-device model has a 4,096-token context per session, and exceeding it throws. Long agent loops need history transforms or summarization, and editing history invalidates the KV cache.
- PCC for developers needs a managed-entitlement application, has a threshold of under 2M first-time downloads for free use, needs a network connection and an Apple Intelligence device, and has a per-user daily quota. Hitting the quota is a hard error until it resets or the user buys iCloud+.
- Background limits: intents get 30 seconds unless they use LongRunningIntent with regular progress. BGContinuedProcessingTask must be user-initiated, visible and cancellable, and the system may kill it. Foundation Models has a background rate budget. No always-on autonomous agent daemon is allowed.
- Guideline 2.5.2 bans downloading or executing code that adds features after review, which kills agents that generate and run new tools or mini-apps on the device (the vibe-coding removals of March 2026).
- Guideline 5.1.2(i) requires in-app disclosure and explicit consent before sending personal data to any third-party AI provider.
- There is no agentic payments API. Apple Pay requires per-transaction user biometric authentication, and there is no delegated spending or mandate for agents on iOS.
- Sending an SMS or iMessage, email or cellular call from a third-party app still requires a user-facing compose or call UI. Autonomous outbound communication means cloud telephony and email or the messaging app's own intents.
- Apple's own agentic features (Passwords auto-change through Safari, Hold Assist, Call Screening, Visual Intelligence camera mode) are not exposed as APIs to third parties.
- Security: Siri can chain intents from multiple apps, which opens a prompt-injection path (calendar events, feeds, tool outputs). Apple expects deterministic confirmations (onToolCall, authenticationPolicy), and a schema's default policy can only be made stricter.
- Third-party model 'Extensions' for Siri, Writing Tools and Image Playground are unconfirmed for developers and not live beyond ChatGPT as of September 2026.
- Vision Pro main-camera frames appear to remain enterprise-only, and Visual Intelligence hands apps a single pixel buffer rather than a continuous feed.

### Whitespace signals
- Cross-app orchestration by independent agents is missing: every agent other than Siri is walled off from other apps' intents. There is room for 'Shortcuts-as-agent-runtime' products and for a user-controlled intent router once MCP-style App Intents ship.
- Personal health coaching: Apple's Mulberry AI coach was delayed and scaled back while HealthKit gained Medications and workout-zone APIs. A privacy-first coach built on Foundation Models or PCC with HealthKit, Watch and Live Activities has a clear opening.
- Agentic commerce on iPhone: Visa, Mastercard, Stripe and Google shipped agent-payment rails in 2026 but Apple has no agent API. Apps that use those rails with a Wallet pass or Live Activity confirmation step are largely unbuilt.
- Tooling for App Intents adoption: MacStories reports patchy third-party coverage in Siri AI and NowSecure says a quarter of apps expose functionality. Generators, testers and security scanners for App Schemas, beyond Apple's AppIntentsTesting, are underserved.
- Prompt-injection defense kits for iOS agents, such as prebuilt historyTransform spotlighting and redaction, onToolCall policy engines, and audit logs, are needed now that Apple has made this the developer's responsibility.
- Siri AI excludes pre-iPhone-15-Pro devices, non-English users, the EU and China. Agent apps that fall back to cloud LLMs, with consent, can serve the large population Apple's agent does not.
- A free PCC tier for apps under 2M downloads, 32K context and Dynamic Profiles make indie on-device plus cloud agents cheap to run, but few apps visibly use multi-profile orchestration yet.
- Phone agents: Apple's Hold Assist and Call Screening show demand, but third parties cannot touch cellular calls. Cloud-telephony agents that call businesses on the user's behalf and report back through Live Activities are a gap.
- Voice agents in CarPlay are newly allowed for every app category in iOS 27. Few vertical in-car agents exist yet, such as a trip concierge, an EV charging planner, or a fleet or field-service agent.
- Visual Intelligence camera-mode integrations for scan-to-act uses (repairs, parts ordering, receipts and expense filing, pantry to grocery order) are available but thinly adopted.
- Watch-first agents: watchOS 27 gains Foundation Models via PCC, so wrist-native agents are possible for the first time.
- Web-task agents like Apple's Passwords auto-change are impossible for third parties. That leaves a possible niche for server-side browser agents paired with iPhone approval UIs (interactive snippets and Live Activities).

## shipped-consumer

As of late September 2026, agentic features on iPhone have moved from demos into mainstream apps, and September 2026 was the biggest month so far. Meta's Muse (launched Sept 8) is a standalone personal-agent app. It sends email, books travel, fills in forms, makes purchases with one-time Stripe Link cards, and since Sept 17 places phone calls to businesses. It took #1 on the US free iPhone chart from ChatGPT with more than 2.5M downloads. The next day OpenAI brought voice-triggered agent work (the 'Work' tab, a cloud browser and finances) to the ChatGPT mobile app. Apple shipped iOS 27 on Sept 14 with Siri AI, which can take actions across apps through App Intents, but only as a gated beta: there is a waitlist, daily usage caps, English only, and no iPhone launch in the EU. The other big players each run an agent product that reaches the iPhone. Google has Gemini Spark (I/O, May 2026) plus AI Mode's agentic restaurant and hotel booking, checkout and business calling. Anthropic has Claude's native iOS integrations (Calendar, Reminders, Health), with Cowork added to mobile (July 2026) and Dispatch for sending tasks from phone to desktop. Perplexity has its iOS Assistant, the Comet browser on iOS (March 2026) and Computer on iOS (March 2026). Microsoft has Copilot Cowork, with approve/reject on mobile. xAI has Grok Bot (Aug 2026). Manus is now owned by Meta, and Genspark's Super Agent includes Call for Me. Almost all of these heavy agents run in cloud sandboxes or cloud browsers and use the iPhone as a remote control and approval screen, because iOS does not let a third-party app operate other apps.

Vertical agents are uneven. The strongest are phone-call and errand agents (Pine AI, Genspark Call for Me, Google's agentic calling, Muse calls), shopping price-watchers (Amazon's Alexa for Shopping, which replaced Rufus in May 2026 and adds Buy for Me and price-triggered auto-buy; Google's agentic checkout and Universal Cart; Phia), money agents (Cleo Autopilot, and ChatGPT finances via Plaid), and meeting capture (Granola on iPhone and Apple Watch, Plaud with more than 2.5M users, Otter). Some are weak on mobile or failing. Most AI calendar and inbox agents are desktop-first or web-first: Motion needs a laptop to schedule and Reclaim has no iOS app. Milo, the family-logistics assistant, shut down in January 2026 because its founder concluded AI was 'too early to be reliable'. Apple shelved its own AI health coach. Messaging has become an important channel for agents. Poke became the first AI agent approved on Apple Messages for Business (June 2026), and OpenClaw's iOS companion app shipped in June 2026. Shopping checkout inside the agent has pulled back: OpenAI dropped Instant Checkout in March 2026 after Walmart measured it converting about 3x worse than a click-through to its own site. Payment and other sensitive steps are usually handed back to the user. Method note: WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy for every domain I tried, so all of these findings come from WebSearch results and snippets, not from opening the pages. Confidence levels reflect that.

### Walls
- iOS gives third-party apps no way to operate other apps' UI. Every heavy consumer agent (Muse, Manus, Grok Bot, Perplexity Computer, ChatGPT agent, Gemini Spark) runs in a cloud sandbox or cloud browser and uses the iPhone as a remote control and approval screen. The only on-device route into other apps is App Intents, which each app must adopt.
- Siri AI (iOS 27) shipped as a beta with a waitlist and daily usage caps, and a paid 'expanded access' tier is coming. It is English only at launch, is not available on iPhone, iPad or Watch in the EU, and needs an iPhone 15 Pro or newer. Third-party agents that build on it inherit these limits.
- Apple deprecated SiriKit at WWDC 2026, so App Intents is now the only way Siri calls into an app. Siri AI can only do as much as each app's App Intents cover, and Apple's own examples are few: WhatsApp, Audible, Outlook, Notability, Tripsy.
- App Store Guideline 2.5.2 (running code that changes the app's own features) was used in March 2026 to block updates for Replit and Vibecode and to pull 'Anything'. Agents that write and run code on the device are effectively banned.
- Guideline 5.1.2(i), in force since Nov 13, 2025: an app must get explicit, revocable consent that names the AI provider and the data shared before sending personal data to a third-party AI.
- Payments hit a trust wall. Gemini Spark stops at checkout and never presses Pay. Muse uses one-time Stripe Link cards. OpenAI dropped ChatGPT Instant Checkout in March 2026 after Walmart saw in-chat checkout convert about 3x worse. Google's agentic checkout runs on Google Pay rather than Apple Pay.
- Calling agents (Pine, Genspark Call for Me, Google agentic calling, Muse calls) all place calls from cloud telephony such as Twilio. Apple's own Hold Assist and Call Screening are built into the Phone app and are not open to third parties.
- Third-party access to iMessage goes only through Apple's Messages for Business, where Poke was the first approved AI agent in June 2026. Claude's iOS integration can create messages and mail but cannot read existing ones.
- CarPlay AI apps (iOS 26.4 and later) are voice-only, have no wake word and cannot control car or iPhone functions.
- Running long jobs in the background pushes work off the phone. Claude Dispatch needs the desktop awake with the app open. ChatGPT scheduled tasks run at most hourly and auto-pause when ignored.
- Many launches are limited by region or plan. Muse is US-only and 18+. ChatGPT Health, finances and medical records are US-only. Claude's Health integration is a US beta for Pro and Max. Gemini Spark needs Pro or Ultra.
- Reliability is still the main blocker for household agents. Milo shut down in January 2026 because small extraction errors made family scheduling more work, not less. CNN's Muse test found a purchase stalled on a store that required a professional license. Siri AI reviews flag weak handling of complex or unavailable actions.
- Sensitive actions (payments, passwords) default to handing control back to the user, so a fully hands-off flow on the phone is rare.

### Whitespace signals
- A reliable family-logistics agent is still missing. Milo quit, saying the technology is 'too early', and the survivors (Ohai) mostly handle lists, SMS and groceries. Reliably pulling details from school emails, coordinating co-parents and checking each step is still open.
- No true mobile-first autonomous calendar agent exists. Motion needs a laptop to schedule, Reclaim has no iOS app, and Howie works through email and iMessage rather than a native app.
- iPhone-native inbox agents are thin. The leaders (Fyxer, Shortwave's AI, Superhuman's AI) are mostly web or browser extensions, and Gmail's AI Inbox is still gated to testers.
- Nothing gives users one place to approve and monitor all their agents. Each one (Muse, ChatGPT, Claude Dispatch, Copilot Cowork, Grok Bot, OpenClaw) has its own approvals and notifications, and there is no cross-agent approval inbox or Live Activity or Watch layer.
- No agent payment system is native to Apple Pay. Agents use Stripe Link one-time cards or Google Pay. A way for users to approve agent purchases with Face ID and spending limits looks unclaimed.
- No third-party agent fully handles calls from the iPhone Phone app. Hold Assist only waits, and calling agents live in the cloud. An agent that waits on hold, then talks, and hands off to the user mid-call inside the phone experience is not available to third parties.
- Apple shelved its AI health coach in February 2026, leaving room for a HealthKit-native coach that acts: booking appointments, reordering prescriptions, adjusting plans. It would not need Oura or Whoop hardware.
- Few App Intents are in the wild so far (Apple's own examples are a handful of apps). Tools or services that help developers expose good App Intents or App Schemas for Siri AI are an opportunity.
- Privacy-first, on-device memory for AI note-taking hardware is open after Meta bought and discontinued Limitless and Amazon bought Bee.
- Language tutors adapt within a conversation but don't plan the learner's week, set up real-world practice or schedule sessions with other people. The agentic tutor is unbuilt.
- EU iPhone users are left out of Siri AI and Muse, which is a market gap for agent apps that comply with EU rules.
- Agentic browsing on iPhone is thin. ChatGPT Atlas has no iOS version, and Comet on iOS is mostly voice, search and research, not full task automation inside Safari.

## builders-oss

As of September 2026 the builder landscape for agentic iOS apps changed a lot at WWDC26 (June 2026). Apple turned the Foundation Models framework into a provider-neutral agent runtime. A new LanguageModel protocol lets one LanguageModelSession drive the on-device model (4K context), Apple's Private Cloud Compute model (32K context, reasoning, free per-user daily quota for eligible small developers), Core AI and MLX local models, and Claude and Gemini through vendor Swift packages (Anthropic shipped anthropics/ClaudeForFoundationModels with App Attest auth and server-side web search/code execution). Dynamic Profiles, ToolCallingMode, history transforms and Apple's open-source foundation-models-utilities (skills, rolling windows, a chat-completions adapter) give indie developers first-party patterns for multi-agent orchestration (baton-pass, phone-a-friend, skills). App Intents added LongRunningIntent, UndoableIntent, confirmation for sensitive entity actions, and app schemas and View Annotations so Siri can act on app content. The open-source layer underneath is mature: MLX Swift / mlx-swift-lm, llama.cpp, whisper.cpp, Argmax WhisperKit/TTSKit, Cactus, Hugging Face AnyLanguageModel and the official MCP Swift SDK. Swift-native agent frameworks built on top of it (AgentRunKit, SwiftAgent, etc.) are still tiny, with tens of stars.

On the product side, the most visible startup energy is in two places. The first is 'personal agent on your iPhone' startups (YC S26 Touchy, which claims actions across 1,000+ apps; YC Moonshot, an always-listening assistant) and the self-hosted OpenClaw ecosystem, whose native iOS node app (June 2026) lets a remote agent drive the phone's camera, location, contacts, calendar and reminders with approvals, subject to strict iOS background limits. The second is 'build apps from your phone' tools: Rork Max, which compiles native Swift on cloud Macs, Bitrig, which generates SwiftUI on the iPhone, and Vibecode. Apple enforced Guideline 2.5.2 against these in March 2026, pushing on-device previews out to external browsers and limiting on-phone generation of Apple-platform software. Developer-side agent infrastructure for iOS is booming: Xcode 26.3/27 agentic coding with Claude/Codex and MCP, XcodeBuildMCP, mobile-mcp and AXe. On-device consumer agents that chain third-party App Intents remain structurally blocked. Only Siri can invoke other apps' intents, there is no system-wide MCP on iOS, and third-party apps cannot automate other apps' UI. That leaves the biggest gaps in cross-app orchestration, durable background agents, and reusable approval, eval and observability kits.

### Walls
- The on-device SystemLanguageModel context is 4,096 tokens per session, including instructions, tool schemas, tool I/O and @Generable schemas. Multi-step agents must split work across sessions (phone-a-friend) or escalate to PCC.
- The PCC model (32K context, reasoning) needs iOS/macOS/watchOS/visionOS 27+, a network connection and an Apple Intelligence-capable device and region. It has a per-user daily quota (iCloud+ upsell) and a managed entitlement. Cost is free only for Small Business Program apps with under 2M first-time downloads.
- The on-device model changes with OS updates (26.4 in February 2026, 27 in June 2026), so prompts and tool-calling behavior must be re-evaluated each release.
- Guideline 2.5.2: apps may not execute code that changes their own or other apps' functionality. In March 2026 Apple used it to block updates for Replit and Vibecode until previews moved to external browsers or on-device generation of Apple-platform apps was removed.
- No system-wide MCP on iOS 27. Apple's MCP support is in Xcode only. Third-party apps cannot invoke other apps' App Intents; only Siri, Shortcuts and Spotlight can. There is no sanctioned cross-app UI automation on device.
- iOS background limits: OpenClaw documents camera, screen and talk commands as blocked when backgrounded and sockets suspended, with only geofence/significant-location events and presence beacons running in the background. LongRunningIntent extends runtime but is still bounded.
- The MCP Swift SDK has no StdioTransport on iOS, so iPhone apps can only reach remote HTTP MCP servers or in-process transports.
- Shipping API keys in apps is unsafe (keys are extractable). Claude offers App Attest device tokens, but they last one hour, carry no end-user identity and do not work through Bedrock, Vertex or Foundry.
- ClaudeForFoundationModels cannot express prompt-caching controls, stop sequences, the Files API, batch or token counting, because Apple's provider protocol has no representation for them.
- Dynamic Profiles trade-off: changing tools or instructions, or rewriting history, invalidates KV caches (higher latency), and retroactive history edits can confuse the model. Apple says to measure with Instruments and the Evaluations framework.
- Large local models (MLC, MLX, llama.cpp) need the increased-memory-limit entitlement and 1 to 8 GB of RAM. Cactus's native Swift bindings are reported as immature.
- Consumer Siri AI features were reported unavailable in the EU and China at launch, while developer APIs ship there. This comes from secondary sources and is unverified.

### Whitespace signals
- There is no dominant Swift-native agent runtime with durable runs, checkpoints, approvals, retries and background resumption. The existing Swift agent SDKs have 15 to 250 stars, compared with Python and TypeScript frameworks that have tens of thousands.
- An App Intents to MCP bridge: exposing an app's App Intents or entities as MCP tools to remote agents (OpenClaw-style gateways, Claude) with on-phone approval. Apple shipped MCP only for Xcode.
- A reusable human-in-the-loop approval UI kit for SwiftUI, Live Activities, Watch and notifications. OpenClaw has approvals, and App Intents now has OwnershipProvidingEntity and UndoableIntent, but no shared component library exists.
- Production observability and evals for on-device agents. Instruments and the Evaluations framework are development-time tools, and no Sentry-style field telemetry for tool-call failures exists for Foundation Models apps.
- A quota- and cost-aware router that goes from on-device to PCC to Claude or Gemini, honoring PCC daily limits and falling back gracefully. The building blocks exist in iOS 27 but no packaged library does.
- Local-first agentic apps: the most-starred open-source local LLM iPhone apps (PocketPal, fullmoon) are chat-only. A private agent that acts on EventKit, HealthKit, Reminders, Photos and Files with MLX or Core AI models is largely unbuilt.
- Voice-first Watch and AirPods agents using on-device ASR/TTS (WhisperKit/TTSKit) plus App Intents are rare. OpenClaw's Watch 'Talk' is one of few.
- Phone-based app builders that generate agent-ready apps (App Intents schemas, Foundation Models tools and profiles) by default. Rork Max mentions Siri Intents, but agent capability is not the headline anywhere.
- Consumer iOS front-ends for the agent-economy infrastructure appearing in YC S26 (agent wallets, phone lines, legal): users need a phone-native place to fund, supervise and audit their agents.

## research-frontier

As of late September 2026 the research frontier for phone agents has split into two tracks that barely touch on iOS. Track one is pixel-level GUI agents: Android benchmarks are close to saturated. MobileWorld's authors report that recent agents exceed 90% on AndroidWorld. Open models such as UI-TARS-2 (73.3), Mobile-Agent-v3 (73.3) and MAI-UI (76.7) have moved the field toward harder long-horizon, cross-app, MCP-augmented and personalized tasks (MobileWorld, KnowU-Bench, MobileMem). Apple's own research (Ferret-UI -> Ferret-UI 2 -> the 3B Ferret-UI Lite) shows a small on-device GUI agent can ground UI well but still fails at multi-step navigation (28% on AndroidWorld). The first native-iOS benchmark, CMU's iOSWorld (June 2026), had to build 26 of its own apps and drive them through Appium/XCUITest in the simulator. The best configuration reaches 52% overall and only 37% on multi-app tasks. That is itself evidence that third-party pixel agents cannot drive real iPhone apps. Track two is structured, sanctioned action surfaces, and this is where iOS actually moved. At WWDC26 Apple rebuilt the on-device model (8K context, now with vision and better tool calling) and added a free-to-small-developers Private Cloud Compute model (32K context, reasoning). Apple also opened the Foundation Models framework to third-party LLMs through a LanguageModel protocol (Anthropic and Google Swift packages were announced, along with MLX and Core AI local backends) and shipped DynamicProfile agent orchestration, Evaluations, and explicit prompt-injection guidance (onToolCall confirmations, spotlighting and redaction, schema-inherited risk and authentication policies). The rebuilt Siri chains actions across apps only through App Intents, App Schemas and view annotations.

Off-device, the protocol layer consolidated quickly. MCP went stateless in its 2026-07-28 spec, with MCP Apps (sandboxed HTML UI) and Tasks as official extensions. A2A reached v1.0 (March 2026) under the Linux Foundation. Commerce now has several overlapping standards: AP2 (mandates, v0.2 'human-not-present', reportedly donated to FIDO), OpenAI and Stripe's ACP (spec 2026-04-17, with MCP compatibility, even though ChatGPT's Instant Checkout was reportedly retired in March 2026), Google and Shopify's UCP (January 2026), and card-network agent tokens (Mastercard Agent Pay, Visa Intelligent Commerce, and Visa inside ChatGPT from June 2026). Apple Pay and Wallet have no agent-payment API in iOS 27. Security research is sobering: environmental and prompt-injection attacks succeed 40-67% of the time on Android agents (MobileWorldSafety), and memory-poisoning attacks on personal agents inject about 98% of the time (GhostWriter). No iOS-specific safety benchmark exists. Voice research is moving to full-duplex speech-to-speech models that delegate tool calls to a text backend. Wearables research (egocentric proactive assistants) is ahead of product: Meta's iOS Wearables Device Access Toolkit (developer preview, v0.9, including Ray-Ban Display output) is the main real bridge from glasses to an iPhone agent. What works in principle, a private on-device agent that sees your screen and acts across apps, is blocked on iOS in practice for everyone except Siri. The buildable path is App Intents + Foundation Models + MCP or remote tools, plus careful confirmation UX.

### Walls
- No iOS equivalent of Android's AccessibilityService: a third-party app cannot read other apps' screens or inject taps. iOS GUI-agent research (iOSWorld) runs only on the simulator through Appium/XCUITest on a Mac with purpose-built apps, so the entire AndroidWorld-style GUI-agent literature does not transfer to shipping iPhone apps.
- Cross-app agentic actions are reserved for Siri: App Intents, App Schemas and view annotations make apps reachable by Siri, but third-party agent apps cannot invoke other apps' intents directly. The user-built Shortcuts app is the only generic bridge.
- On-device AFM context is 8,192 tokens and the model is about 3B parameters and not meant for world knowledge. Longer agent loops must use the 32K PCC model, a third-party cloud model or a larger local model, which brings privacy and latency tradeoffs.
- Sustained local LLM inference on iPhone hits thermal throttling and RAM ceilings. Third-party claims of 30-40 tok/s for 2-4B models on iPhone 17 Pro assume a cool phone, and long agent sessions or full-duplex speech degrade.
- Background execution limits: iOS suspends apps, so long-running autonomous agents cannot keep running on the device. They need server-side execution with push notifications, MCP Tasks or A2A tasks polled later, or user-initiated background work.
- No third-party always-on wake word or continuous background camera. Ambient voice or vision agents must keep a foreground or audio session active or rely on external wearables (for example the Meta DAT SDK), with visible recording indicators.
- Apple Pay and Wallet expose no agent-delegation or mandate API in iOS 27, and each Apple Pay authorization needs user presence. Agent commerce on iOS must use Stripe, Visa or Mastercard agent tokens, and App Store in-app-purchase rules still apply to digital goods.
- Prompt and environmental injection is unsolved. Android agents show 40-67% attack success (MobileWorldSafety) and memory poisoning about 98% injection (GhostWriter). Apple's mitigations (onToolCall confirmations, spotlighting, redaction, auth policies) reduce the risk but are partly probabilistic.
- MCP Apps renders HTML in sandboxed iframes, and native widgets are explicitly deferred. iOS hosts must use WKWebView, and nothing maps cleanly to SwiftUI, Live Activities or widgets.
- Apple's WWDC26 agent APIs (Foundation Models, App Intents) do not mention MCP or A2A, so interop with the broader agent-protocol ecosystem is left to developers.

### Whitespace signals
- No iOS-specific agent safety or injection benchmark exists (all of GhostEI-Bench, MIRAGE, MobileWorldSafety and AnTrap are Android). An iOSWorld-style safety suite built on App Intents and Foundation Models tool calls is open.
- An App Intents <-> MCP bridge: expose an app's App Intents and entities as an MCP server, or let an in-app Foundation Models agent consume remote MCP servers with confirmation gates. Apple did not ship this.
- A mandate-signing 'agent wallet' on iPhone: Secure Enclave, passkeys and Face ID to sign AP2 or Verifiable Intent mandates and issue scoped Visa or Mastercard agent tokens, filling the gap Apple Pay leaves.
- Hybrid device-cloud router apps using the iOS 27 LanguageModel protocol (on-device AFM or Gemma 4 for private steps; PCC, Claude or Gemini for reasoning), a pattern MAI-UI reports cuts cloud calls by 40% or more.
- Personal-memory agents with memory governance (write policies and retrieval screens like AM-Sentry) built on the Spotlight semantic index and the FM Spotlight tool, evaluated against MobileMem-style year-long tasks.
- A full-duplex voice front end with a delegated tool-calling backend for iPhone and AirPods (the research pattern from 2609.19334 and OpenAI's chat-supervisor), with confirmations pushed to Apple Watch.
- Glasses-to-iPhone agents built on Meta's DAT SDK (camera stream, Ray-Ban Display output) plus an on-device VLM, for hands-free task agents such as cooking, repair and shopping.

## demand-whitespace

Demand for agentic help is real but lopsided. Menlo Ventures' July 2026 survey (5,067 US adults) finds AI use flat at 64% while spend tripled to about $40B. 41% of AI users have tried an agent and 24% use one regularly, but only 20% have given an agent access to a financial account, against 36% for email. Agent use is mostly among payers (65% vs 13% of non-payers). Commerce surveys show the same pattern: consumers let AI advise, but most want approval gates, budget caps and easy returns before an agent buys for them. Pew still finds half of Americans more concerned than excited. So the gap is less about capability and more about bounded, auditable delegation in high-stakes, messy, recurring chores. Menlo itself names money, healthcare and family logistics as problems 'people deal with every week, but still haven't handed off to AI.' On iOS, Apple's Siri AI shipped with iOS 27 in September 2026 after an 18-month delay. It launched behind a waitlist, in English only, with daily cloud caps, no EU or China availability, and only a small set of App Intents partners, so third-party vertical agents still have room.

The clearest whitespace is on the consumer side of systems that already have AI on the institution's side. Prior authorization and appeal AI is sold to providers, not patients, even though only 11.5% of denials are appealed and about 80% of appeals succeed. Immigration AI is sold mostly to lawyers, while consumer apps stop at explaining form fields. Medical-bill tools write letters but leave the calls and follow-up to the patient. Caregiver apps track medications and appointments but do not act. ADHD planners remind but do not do the dreaded admin. Blind and low-vision users get scene description (Be My Eyes, Seeing AI), but research shows computer-use agents fail badly under assistive-tech conditions and override user choices. On iPhone, the sandbox blocks a generic agent that drives other apps: gig-driver auto-accept tools such as Maxymo keep full automation on Android. Winning iOS designs will therefore likely work outside the phone's UI (phone calls, email, portals, messaging-native agents like the OpenClaw pattern) or through App Intents, with explicit approval steps and an action log.
